Overview

Food & Friends is a cook's journal for iPhone. You write down recipes, plan weekly menus, keep a shopping list, and share what you're cooking with friends at The Table. This page sets out what data the app collects, why, where it is kept, and who else sees it.

The short version: we keep what you write in the app so it's there when you come back and so your friends can see what you choose to share. There is no advertising, no tracking, and no analytics in the app, and we do not sell your data to anyone.

What we collect

  • Account information. Your email address and the sign-in method you used (email and password, Sign in with Apple, or Google Sign-In). If you use Sign in with Apple and choose to hide your email, we receive Apple's relay address instead of your own.
  • Profile. Your username, an optional display name, a city and state you type in or fill in with "Find me", an optional profile photo, your friend list, and, if you pick one, your Kroger store.
  • What you write. Recipes and their photos, your variations and notes on recipes, weekly menus, shopping lists, and friend requests you send or answer.
  • Activity. Short entries such as "wrote down a recipe" or "planned a menu", created when you do those things, so your friends can see them at The Table.
  • Reports and blocks. If you report a recipe, menu, entry, or person, we keep the report (who sent it, what it was about, and the reason given). If you block someone, we keep that so their content stays hidden from you.

Location

During profile setup there is a "Find me" button. Only if you tap it, the app asks iOS for your location once and turns it into a city and state (for example "Cincinnati, OH") using Apple's location services. That city and state are saved on your profile. Your coordinates are not stored and are not sent to us. You can skip "Find me" and type a city, or leave it blank.

What we don't collect

No advertising identifiers, no analytics or tracking SDKs, no contacts, no browsing history, and no payment information. If you have chosen to share crash reports with developers in your iPhone's settings, Apple may pass anonymous crash logs on to us; that is controlled by Apple, not the app.

How we use it

  • To run the app: keep your recipes, menus, and lists, and show them to you on any device you sign in on.
  • To show your profile, the recipes you share, and your activity to the people you've chosen as friends, and to let people find you by username to send a friend request.
  • To review reports and keep The Table free of objectionable content and abusive users.
  • To answer you when you write to us.

We do not sell your data, share it with advertisers or data brokers, or use it to track you across other companies' apps and websites.

Who can see what

  • Recipes are either kept among friends (you and the people you've added as friends) or shared with everyone who uses the app. You choose for each recipe.
  • Activity entries appear to your friends at The Table.
  • Your profile (username, display name, city, photo) can be seen by other people using the app, so friends can find you.
  • Your shopping lists and your Kroger store are for you; the app does not show them to other people.
  • Blocking someone ends your friendship and hides their content from you.

Where it's stored

Your account and everything you write are stored in Google Firebase (Authentication, Cloud Firestore, Cloud Storage, and Cloud Functions), run by Google LLC on Google Cloud infrastructure in the United States, and subject to Google's privacy and security terms for Firebase. Data travels between the app and Firebase over encrypted connections (HTTPS/TLS).

Other services

  • Google Firebase holds your account and the things you write, as described above.
  • Apple and Google handle sign-in if you choose Sign in with Apple or Google Sign-In. They tell us your email address (or Apple's relay address) and an account identifier; they don't receive your recipes.
  • Kroger. When you pick a Kroger store or match shopping-list items to products, your phone sends the ZIP code or store link you entered and the item search terms (for example "flour") directly to Kroger's public product API to look up stores, products, and prices. No Kroger account is linked, and nothing else about you is sent. Kroger's handling of those requests is covered by Kroger's privacy policy.

Your choices

  • Edit or delete what you've written. Recipes, variations, and menus can be changed or deleted in the app, and shopping-list lines can be taken off at any time.
  • Choose who sees each recipe: just friends, or everyone.
  • Remove friends or block people from their profile.
  • Turn off location for Food & Friends in your iPhone's Settings at any time. The app only asks when you tap "Find me".

Deleting your account

In the app, open You → Delete account. This permanently deletes your profile, recipes, variations, menus, shopping lists, activity entries, friend links, the photos you uploaded, and your sign-in account. It cannot be undone.

Reports you filed about other people's content are kept after you leave, because they are part of the moderation record. You can also ask us to delete your account by emailing david.lonneman@gmail.com from the address on the account.

How long we keep it

We keep your data for as long as you have an account. When you delete your account, it is removed from our database and storage. Copies in Firebase's own backups expire on Google's normal schedule.

Children

Food & Friends is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them. If you believe a child under 13 has made an account, email us and we will delete it.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes in a way that matters, we'll say so in the app or on this page, and update the effective date at the top.

Contact

Questions, concerns, or deletion requests: david.lonneman@gmail.com.
